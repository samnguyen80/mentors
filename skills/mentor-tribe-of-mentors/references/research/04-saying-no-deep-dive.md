# 04 · Deep dive on one question: how to say "no" (Q10)

Sources:
- https://tim.blog/2018/02/03/the-tim-ferriss-show-transcripts-how-to-say-no/ (and the original https://tim.blog/2017/11/25/how-to-say-no/) — episode #282 on tim.blog.

- Secondary, for cross-reference only: https://www.artofmanliness.com/character/manly-lessons/tribe-of-mentors-tim-ferriss-interview/ (Art of Manliness podcast interview with Tim).

## Principles distilled
1. Once you have had some success, your default should be "no" to almost everything. Derek Sivers: if it is not a "hell yes", it is a "no".
2. Turn refusal into a policy, not a judgment of the person in front of you (a billionaire declined Tim under a "no meetings for a quarter" policy; Neal Stephenson declines across the board because the to-do list keeps breeding).
3. Be clear and do not leave hope dangling ("ping me in 3 months" is a poor way to decline).
4. Be warm and grateful: Wendy MacNaughton said she was resting after years of back-to-back work and kept her tone personal; Danny Meyer thanked the person and wished the project well.
5. You can recommend a replacement (Wendy suggested other people, many of whom ended up in the book).
6. Accept that people may take offense; a polite refusal usually earns respect later.

## Specific filters from individual mentors
- Kyle Maynard: rate an opportunity 1-10 and never allow a 7 (a 7 is usually guilt or fear of missing out).
- Esther Dyson / Tim O'Reilly: "If this event were happening next Tuesday instead of months from now, would I still accept?" (reported via secondary sources).
- Dustin Moskovitz, Aaron Sorkin: decline without giving a reason, so as not to invite debate (secondary source).
- Naval Ravikant: decline almost everything, choosing only people he can work with indefinitely and work that is itself a joy.
- Steven Pressfield: looks at mail the way a celebrity's security company does, turning away nearly all of it.
- Adam Robinson: the filter is a personal mission.
- Irene Latham: asks "Do I WANT to? Will it be fun?" and weighs the real cost, which is time with family.
- Terry Crews: let people leave your life; "thinning the crowd" of relationships.
