# 02 · Four publicly excerpted profiles: Terry Crews, Steven Pressfield, Bozoma Saint John, Naval Ravikant

Source: https://tim.blog/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Tools-of-Titans-and-Tribe-of-Mentors-Introductions-and-Profiles.pdf (published by Tim Ferriss himself as an excerpt). What follows is a summary in the skill author's own words; short quotes carry the speaker's name.

## Terry Crews (actor, former NFL player)
- Book: The Master Key System (Haanel) teaches him to visualize what he wants as already done and to focus like a laser; to "have" you must "do", and to "do" you must "be". He rereads it every month. Also Man's Search for Meaning and You Are Not So Smart.
- Favorite failure: the regional final in his senior year of high school; the opposing team was stalling, he stole the ball, ran the full length of the court and missed the shot at the buzzer, and was ridiculed by the press and the school. A few days later he realized: "I took the shot." From then on he decided he would succeed or fail on his own terms, not leave it to other people.
- Billboard: an Emerson line, roughly that great achievements do not come from cowards. He asks himself "what is the worst that could happen" and weighs it against living safely in a life he hates.
- Advice to students: smart is not the same as wise; smart is following the GPS all the way into the ocean, wise is seeing the turn toward the ocean and choosing another road. Ignore every voice saying "you'll miss out"; his mistakes in business and marriage all came from fear of missing out.
- Bad advice: "work hard to beat your competition." He thinks competition is the opposite of creativity; when you work from inspiration there is no opponent, and you can cheer others on.
- Saying no: let people leave your life; every relationship must be voluntary on both sides. He is "thinning the crowd": one wrong person in your circle can wreck your whole future. Tip: ask your imaginary great-grandchildren which relationships to keep and which to leave.

## Steven Pressfield (author of The War of Art)
- Most influential book: History of the Peloponnesian War (Thucydides), which sees people exactly as they are, with no "isms".
- Failure: wrote novels for 15 years without publishing, drove a taxi in New York, moved to Hollywood to work as a screenwriter; that "working" stretch trained the professional writer he later became.
- Billboard: he would put up no billboard, and would take down everyone else's.
- Investment: only betting on himself (two years on a book that could fail) and "investing with the heart".
- Habit: group workouts with a trainer who says "nothing today is harder than the workout you just finished."
- Saying no: he looks at mail and invitations the way a celebrity's security company would: nearly all of it gets turned away.
- Overwhelm: Jack LaLanne allowed a day off but you could not eat that day, implying you must not lose focus for long; one decent day of work brings you back to yourself.
- Bad advice in his field: build a "platform", grow a following, tricks for gaming the system: all of it is surface. A memorable line: "We're like the Platte River, a mile wide and an inch deep." The real work is going deep and staying there for a very long time.
- Advice to students: go out into the real world, fail early to learn who you are; you have dozens of lives ahead, so do not fear friends "finishing first".

## Bozoma Saint John (brand executive, formerly Apple Music/Uber)
- Book: Song of Solomon (Toni Morrison): read slowly, reread; the lesson that people who truly care will go the extra mile to understand you.
- Habit: people-watching in public places to understand culture.
- Overwhelm: a 20-minute nap as a "refresh" button; how you feel on waking is an instinctive decision, because by then you have stopped overthinking.
- Billboard: "Be the change you want to see in the world" (and "starting with the man in the mirror"): complain less, and remember we have the power to change things.

## Naval Ravikant (AngelList)
- Books: Total Freedom (Krishnamurti), Sapiens (Harari), the books of Matt Ridley.
- Failure: suffering is the moment of clarity that forces change; childhood poverty led to learning how to make money, losing faith in bosses led to independence, nearly marrying the wrong person helped him recognize the right one.
- Billboard: "Desire is a contract that you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want." Pick one big desire, pick it carefully, and drop the small ones.
- Investment: every book he read without being assigned or having a purpose; love of reading is a superpower, and what is scarce is the desire to learn, not the means of learning.
- New habit: happiness is a choice and a skill; the mind is as plastic as the body, and the voice in your head is not an absolute source of truth.
- Advice to students: follow curiosity, not what is "hot"; do everything with less anxiety; ignore the news, complainers and high-conflict people; do not do something ethically wrong, because you are watching yourself: "Self-esteem is just the reputation that you have with yourself."
- Bad advice: "you are too young"; real learning happens by doing.
- Saying no: to almost everything; work only with people you can work with for the long run, only on work that is itself a joy, and think long-term.
- Overwhelm: memento mori (remember that we will die).
