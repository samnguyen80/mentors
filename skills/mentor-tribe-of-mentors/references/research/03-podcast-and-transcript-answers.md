Note: these are answers given on the podcast to the same set of questions; the wording does not necessarily match the chapters in the book.

## Soman Chainani (author)
- Overwhelm: check the body first (needs movement, circulation); often the problem is a task list that is too long, so clear the calendar and cut commitments. Loss of focus is often fear, especially at the start of a book.
- Failure: his graduation film was criticized by professors; the night before the review he re-cut it to their taste and the panel panned that version; a teacher who later saw the original said he could see the person behind it. Lesson: trust your own work over outside pressure.
- Saying no: while writing a book he turns down other creative projects to give it everything; the yes/no decision rests on the core need (making something that lasts).
- Advice: every day should hold something you look forward to; be wary of taking a job you do not care about as a "stepping stone".
- Bad advice: artists should not try to live purely off their art; having other income reduces money pressure and helps you write better.
- Billboard (in Hollywood): "They're lying."

## Susan Cain (author of Quiet)
- Failure: was told she would not make partner at a law firm, left, and returned to her dream of writing; her first Quiet manuscript was rejected by an editor, and then she rewrote it.
- Advice: do not tie creative work to financial desperation; build a sustainable financial "cushion" so creative time is focused and enjoyable.
- Habit: one latte a day, reserved for her most creative writing hour so the drink becomes linked to writing. She likes sad music, in minor keys.

## Graham Duncan (investor)
- Overwhelm: asks himself "what is the worst that could happen if things don't go to plan"; uses Byron Katie's question "could the opposite of my story be true?"
- Failure: in 2007 he passed on an investment because a former boss gave a lukewarm recommendation, and the investment later did very well; he learned to hold contradictory information more lightly.
- Career advice: swim between the two banks of "chaos" and "rigidity" (Dan Siegel's model): early in a career learn the conventions of the field, then gradually develop your own voice and risk tolerance.
- Habit: morning swims, "feeling the water" before pulling with the arms.
- Billboard (words of Kwame Anthony Appiah): the issue is not how well you play but deciding which game to play.

## Adam Robinson (investor, coach)
- Billboard: four sentences; the one he likes best reminds him to change his approach when results do not improve.
- Bad advice (finance): gathering more information often raises confidence without raising accuracy (he cites the 1974 study on horse-race prediction).
- Best investment: learning meditation through heart rate variability (HRV) biofeedback, with brief pauses to breathe many times a day.
- New habit: paying more attention to people than to ideas.
- Overwhelm: asks himself whether he is acting like his best self and what he can drop to regain control; keeps one day a week empty as a cushion; slows down and postpones the least essential commitment.
- Saying no: uses a personal mission as the filter; anything that does not advance the core goal is declined.
- Advice to students: ask "who do you want to become" rather than only "what do you want to do"; things like love and success often arrive indirectly.

## Tim Ferriss answers for himself (episode #284), key points
- Overwhelm: a body checklist (have I eaten enough, too much caffeine?), an 80/20 analysis on paper, and asking "what would this look like if it were easy".
- Billboard: you are the average of the five people you spend the most time with.
- Failure: The 4-Hour Chef sold poorly and burned him out, but it led him to start the podcast, which ended up more successful than the books.
- Writing books: only do it when he can make it his number one priority for a year.
- Focus: rank opportunities from 1 to 10 and never choose a 7 (borrowed from Kyle Maynard); the "Hell yes or no" filter (Derek Sivers); decline by blanket policy (no meetings for a month, no conferences).
- Odd habits: phone in airplane mode all morning; superstitions (no red ink, the number 555).
