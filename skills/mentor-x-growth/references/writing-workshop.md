## How to Write Short Tweets

**Best for**: validating ideas, everyday engagement, putting out opinions

**Format options**:
- Opinion statement: one clear stance ("Most people are doing X wrong...")
- Golden line: a single sentence that can be screenshotted and shared
- Question: provokes replies (remember Reply=27x Like)
- Personal redefinition: "X isn't Y, X is Z"

**Dickie Bush's mix**: 75% breadth content (viral-friendly, can spread no matter who writes it) + 25% depth content (personal stories/unique perspectives, building recognizability)

## Hook Improvement Examples (Before → After)

**Example 1: Promoting AI tools**
- Before: `I tested 5 AI coding tools. Here's what I found.`
- Problems: no curiosity gap, no credibility anchor, no specific benefit
- After: `I mass-tested 5 AI coding tools on the same project (a full-stack app in 48 hours). One saved me 12 hours. The others were useless. A thread:`
- What changed: added a specific scenario (full-stack app), a time anchor (48h), suspense (which one?), credibility (measured data)

**Example 2: Build in Public**
- Before: `Just launched my new app. Check it out!`
- Problems: zero curiosity gap, self-centered, doesn't answer "why should I look"
- After: `I built an iOS app with zero coding experience using only AI tools. It hit #1 Paid on the App Store in 3 days. Here's exactly how (and what almost killed it):`
- What changed: added an identity contrast (zero experience), a result anchor (#1 Paid), a time frame (3 days), suspense (almost killed it)

**Example 3: Controversial opinion**
- Before: `AI coding tools are overrated.`
- Problems: the opinion is too vague, no stake in the ground
- After: `Unpopular opinion: 90% of "AI coding tools" reviews on X are from people who never shipped a real product. I've shipped 3 apps. Here's what actually works vs what's just demo-ware:`
- What changed: added specific data (90%), credibility (shipped 3 apps), an opposing side (demo-ware), a promised payoff (what works)

## How to Write Threads

**Four-part structure** (Cole/Bush):

**1. Hook (opening)**
Must answer: Who is it for? What is it about? Why should they trust you? What will they get?

Hook formula:
- Credibility element ("I studied 1000...", "As a Y with X years of...")
- Scene anchor ("In 2024...", "Last week...")
- Core benefit ("How to...", "Why...")
- Specific deliverable ("10 bullets on...", "A thread:")

**2. Main Points (body)**
- Treat the first sentence of each tweet as a subheading
- 1/3/1 rhythm
- Each one can stand alone
- Sweet spot: 8-12 tweets

**3. TL;DR**
- Write the TL;DR first as an outline
- List only the headline points

**4. CTA (call to action)**
- Summarize the core takeaways
- State the next step clearly (Follow/Bookmark/Newsletter)

**Cole's 7 templates**:
1. **Framework**: To solve X, I do Y, To achieve Z
2. **Story**: give the ending first → then the beginning → readers finish to find the "middle"
3. **Actionable**: a list of actionable advice
4. **Curation**: "I read everything {name} has ever written; here are the best N"
5. **Lessons**: extract lessons from personal experience
6. **Mistakes**: "X mistakes I made doing Y"
7. **Contrarian**: challenge common beliefs

## AI-Niche-Specific Thread Types

1. "I tested X and the result was surprising": hands-on tests of new models/tools
2. "N lessons from 0 to $XK MRR": Build in Public roundups
3. "This paper changed my thinking": paper explainers
4. "X vs Y: an in-depth comparison": tool/model head-to-head reviews
5. "I used AI to do X and saved N hours": hands-on case studies

## Topic Selection System

**Endless Idea Generator** (Cole/Bush, 100+ topics in 30 minutes):

**Step 1: 2-Year Test**
Ask yourself: What problems have I solved over the past two years? What have I learned? List 3-5 topic buckets.

**Step 2: Add qualifiers**
Add specific qualifiers to a topic until it's "uncomfortably specific". Target reader = yourself two years ago.

**Step 3: 4A × topic = matrix**
One topic × 4 angles (Actionable/Analytical/Aspirational/Anthropological) × multiple formats = hundreds of pieces of content.

**Step 4: Pick the 3 that excite you most and start writing**

**Bush's big-vs-small question strategy**:
- Big questions (how to be happier) = big audience but shallow → pull in new people
- Small questions (how to grow podcast from 2K to 10K) = small audience but deep → retain people
