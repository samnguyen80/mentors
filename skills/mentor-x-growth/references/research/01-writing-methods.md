## 3. Core Frameworks and Formulas (Actionable)

### 3.1 Topic Selection System: Endless Idea Generator

**Source**: [Dickie Bush on X](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1577293300570247168) (primary) + [Ship 30 Idea Generator](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-generate-112-new-content-ideas-in-30-minutes) (primary)

A four-step process that claims to produce 100+ topics in 30 minutes:

**Step 1: The 2-Year Test (find your topics)**
- Ask yourself: What problems have I solved in the past two years? What have I learned?
- List every answer freely, without judging
- Narrow down to 3-5 topic buckets
- Pick the 3 that excite you most

**Step 2: Add Specificity (add qualifiers)**
- Add concrete qualifiers to the topic until it is "uncomfortably specific," then add one more layer
- Qualifier dimensions: industry, audience, region, platform, price point, distribution method
- Principle: "The qualifiers you add should turn the target reader into you from two years ago"
- Example: money → investing → investing in your 20s → investing in your 20s with no financial background

**Step 3: 4A Framework × Proven Approaches (generate a headline matrix)**

The 4A are content angles:
| Angle | Meaning | Common formats |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| **Actionable** | Teach the reader how to do something | Tips, Hacks, Resources, Guides, How-to |
| **Analytical** | Back it up with data / analysis | Stats, Trends, Reasons, Numbers, Frameworks |
| **Aspirational** | Inspiration + possibility | Lessons, Mistakes, Habits, Reflections |
| **Anthropological** | Touch on universal human nature | Fears, Failures, Lies, Struggles |

Example, with the topic "building a daily writing habit":
- Actionable: "X mistakes to avoid when building a daily writing habit"
- Analytical: "Why making it to Day X is the key to building a writing habit"
- Aspirational: "3 lessons I learned from writing for 600 days in a row"
- Anthropological: "The #1 reason people stop writing shortly after starting"

**Step 4: Choose 3 Ideas (pick what to execute)**
- Pick the 3 from the matrix that excite you most
- Start writing. "When you sit down to write the idea that made your eyes light up, it feels effortless"

**Key insight**: One topic × 4A × many formats = hundreds of pieces of content. Topic selection is not an inspiration problem, it is a combinatorial problem.

### 3.2 Headline Formulas: Headline Architecture

**Source**: [5 Pillars of Digital Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-5-pillars-of-digital-writing) (primary) + [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

**Three questions every headline must answer**:
1. WHO: Who is this written for?
2. WHAT: What is it about?
3. WHY: Why should the reader read it? (the promise / benefit)

**5-element checklist**:
1. How many? (a number creates a container)
2. WHAT? (precise wording)
3. WHO? (the narrower the better)
4. FEEL (emotional tone)
5. Outcome/Promise (what the reader gains)

**Headline Checklist (5 progressive steps)**:
1. Be CLEAR, not Clever: no wordplay, say directly what the piece is
2. Specify the WHAT: use precise terms (save vs. invest vs. make money are different things)
3. Specify the WHO: narrow until it is uncomfortable (e.g. "junior accountants at medical software companies")
4. Specify the WHY: spell out what the reader gets
5. Twist The Knife: add one more layer of sweetener / surprise

**10 high-performing headline formats**:
1. Big numbers ("I studied 1,000...")
2. Dollar signs ("How I turned $100 into...")
3. Credible names ("Warren Buffett's...")
4. "Just happened" ("This just happened...")
5. Questions ("Why do most writers fail?")
6. Success stories ("How I went from X to Y")
7. Unexpected combinations ("What poker taught me about writing")
8. Industry-specific ("For SaaS founders...")
9. Nested (a thing inside a thing) ("The writing mistake that kills your investing")
10. X-item lists ("7 ways to...")

**Progressive Intensification Pattern**:
- Base: "How to generate 100 ideas"
- + time constraint: "in 30 minutes"
- + obstacle overcome: "even if you think you have nothing to say"

### 3.3 Thread Writing Formula

**Source**: [How to Write a Twitter Thread](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-a-twitter-thread) (primary) + [Nicolas Cole 7 Templates Tweet](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1483922200662970370) (primary)

#### Four-part structure: Lead-In → Main Points → TL;DR → CTA

**1. Lead-In Tweet (the opening hook)**
It must answer:
- Who is this written for?
- What is it about?
- Why should they trust you?
- What will the reader get?

Hook formula structure:
- A credibility element ("One of the most legendary...")
- A time / scene anchor ("In 1982...")
- The core benefit ("How to write...")
- A specific deliverable ("10 bullets on effective writing")

**Pro tip**: Top creators write 10-15 versions of the hook before publishing

**2. Main Points (the body)**
- Treat the first sentence of each tweet as a "subheading"
- Use bullet points instead of paragraphs
- 1/3/1 rhythm: one opening sentence → three points / examples → one transition sentence
- Every tweet must stand on its own
- Sweet spot: 5-7 tweets (not counting the final CTA)

**3. TL;DR Recap**
- Write the TL;DR first and use it as the outline
- List only the headings of the main points
- Let readers skim the whole picture

**4. CTA (call to action)**
- Summarize the core takeaway
- Reinforce the value already delivered
- Tell the reader clearly what to do next
- Template: "If you enjoyed this thread, follow me for more like this"
- Cole's metaphor: the CTA is the "gift shop." After the reader has walked through the exhibit, you steer them to the shop at the exit

#### Nicolas Cole's 7 Thread Templates

**Source**: [Nicolas Cole Tweet](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1483922200662970370) (primary) + [Typeshare Templates](https://typeshare.co/templates/pack/nicolas-cole-thread-hooks) (primary tool)

Cole claims that across his 200+ threads he mostly uses only these 7 templates. The ones confirmed so far:

1. **Framework Thread**: Formula: To solve X [a hard problem], I do Y [an unconventional approach], To achieve Z [a desirable outcome]
2. **Story Thread**: Structure: ending → beginning → "read on to find the middle"
3. **Actionable Takeaways Thread**: List style, with each tweet giving one actionable tip
4. **Curation Thread**: "I've read everything {creator} has done for the last {timeframe}. These are the best threads on {topic}."
5. **Lessons Learned Thread**: Extract lessons from personal experience
6. **Mistakes Thread**: "X mistakes I made doing Y"
7. **Contrarian Thread**: Challenge a common belief

(Note: The full details of all 7 templates are in Cole's original Twitter thread. Some template names are inferred from Ship 30 teaching material and the Typeshare template pack.)

#### Three high-performing thread types

**Source**: [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

1. **Stories**: Give the ending first → then the beginning → readers read to the end to find the "middle"
2. **Frameworks**: Curated or original thinking systems with a clear reader benefit
3. **Actionable Takeaways**: A list for achieving a specific result

### 3.4 Formatting Rhythm System

**Source**: [5 Pillars](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-5-pillars-of-digital-writing) (primary) + [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

#### The 1/3/1 writing rhythm

Core idea: "If your writing can't be skimmed, it can't be read."

**1/3/1 structure**:
- 1 hook sentence to open
- 3 sentences of elaboration (pulling the reader deeper)
- 1 transition / summary sentence

Variations: 1/4/1, 1/5/1, 1/2/5/2/1 (like music, crescendo → decrescendo)

**Why it works**: Single-sentence lines act as "checkpoints," giving the reader small dopamine hits that encourage them to keep reading

#### Six types of paragraph openers

1. A strong declarative statement
2. A thought-provoking question
3. A counterintuitive view
4. A moment in time
5. A vulnerable confession
6. A strange / unique insight

#### Core formatting principles
- Turn paragraphs into bullet lists
- Open every section with a single sentence
- The "big wheel and spokes" structure: the H1 headline is the wheel, and the H2/H3 subheadings are the spokes
- Cole's own words: "Formatting is the easiest 10x improvement you can make in your writing."

### 3.5 Lean Writing Content Expansion System

**Source**: [Lean Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/lean-writing-on-twitter-how-to-turn-a-tweet-into-a-thread-into-an-atomic-essay) (primary) + [Lean Writing Method](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-lean-writing-method-how-to-expand-short-form-content-into-longer-form-assets) (primary)

#### Expansion path
```
Tweet → Atomic Essay → Twitter Thread → LinkedIn Post → Blog/Ultimate Guide → Email Course → Digital Product → Online Course → Business
```

#### Three-step method
1. **Start Small**: Test the idea in public, in the smallest form (a single tweet)
2. **Expand What Works**: Once the data feedback shows it works, expand it into a longer format
3. **Expand Again**: Write about the same successful topic repeatedly, from multiple angles, on multiple platforms

#### 10 expansion angles (turn one article into ten)

**Source**: [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)

Dickie Bush suggests: Tips, Stats, Steps, Lessons, Benefits, Reasons, Mistakes, Examples, Questions, Personal Stories

**Source**: [Nicolas Cole Medium](https://nicolascole77.medium.com/the-ultimate-online-writing-republishing-framework-24028e37427d) (primary) + [Cole Tweet Thread](https://twunroll.com/article/1361689241978163202) (secondary archive)

1. Write Atomic Essay
2. Post image on Twitter
3. (Bonus) Copy/paste text as thread
4. Find relevant Question on Quora and copy/paste + image
5. Copy/paste on Medium
6. Copy/paste on LinkedIn

Core insight: The same content reaches different audiences on different platforms. It is not "posting the same thing again," it is "reaching different people."

### 3.8 Hook-Story-Offer Framework

**Source**: [Ship 30 Hook-Story-Offer](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-hook-story-offer-framework-an-easy-copywriting-formula-for-beginners) (primary)

Four-step formula:
1. **Name The Benefit**: What the reader can gain (mental clarity / financial freedom, etc.)
2. **Put The Benefit In An Unexpected Setting**: Create contrast (a weight-loss article set in a Pizza Hut)
3. **Frame As Personal Story**: Personalize the hook with "I..." or "How I..."
4. **Edit For Clarity and Credibility**: Make sure it is believable and clear

Example: "How I lost 10lbs in 4 weeks sitting in Pizza Hut"

## 4. When to Use Short Tweets vs. Long Threads

### Sources
- [Lean Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/lean-writing-on-twitter-how-to-turn-a-tweet-into-a-thread-into-an-atomic-essay) (primary)
- [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)
- [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)

### Single Tweet (short tweet)
- Purpose: **Validate an idea.** The smallest possible investment to test whether a point resonates
- Types: Opinion statements, punchlines, one-sentence frameworks, questions
- Strategy: Post several a day and watch which one performs best
- Cole calls it the "atomic unit" of the content system

### Atomic Essay (250-word mini-essay)
- Purpose: **Go deep on one point.** More complete than a tweet, but without much investment
- Publishing format: Post a screenshot on Twitter + a text version as a thread
- Advantage: Structured practice that forces you to make one point clearly in 250 words

### Twitter Thread (long thread)
- Purpose: **Show depth + drive large-scale growth.** Threads are the strongest growth engine on Twitter
- Sweet spot: 5-7 tweets
- Timing: When a short tweet or atomic essay performs well, expand it into a thread
- Structure: Must have a hook + structured body + TL;DR + CTA

### Dickie Bush's content mix strategy

**Source**: [Growth In Reverse](https://growthinreverse.com/dickie-bush/) (secondary analysis) + [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)

- **75% Reach Content (breadth content)**: Viral-friendly content on broad topics that spreads no matter who writes it
- **25% Resonance Content (depth content)**: Personal stories / unique perspectives that build the author's recognizability and trust
- Breadth content pulls in new followers; depth content keeps them

## 5. Category Design Writing Positioning

### Sources
- [Snow Leopard Book Notes](https://www.danielscrivner.com/notes/snow-leopard-nicolas-cole) (secondary detailed notes)
- [Outliers Podcast #140](https://www.outlieracademy.com/episode/140) (primary podcast interview)

### Content Pyramid
A five-level evolution model:

| Level | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| L1 | Consumption | Passively consuming content |
| L2 | Curation | Organizing and presenting other people's ideas (e.g. Tim Ferriss-style interviews) |
| L3 | Creation (Obvious) | Original but linear / predictable content, an A→B connection |
| L4 | Creation (Non-Obvious) | Cross-domain insights connecting unrelated fields (e.g. using esports experience to explain writing technique) |
| L5 | Category Creation | Creating an entirely new framework that redefines how the audience thinks |

### Disco Party vs Pool Party
Don't enter an existing market and say "I'm better" (a pool party). Create an entirely new option (a disco party). Let the audience choose between two different experiences, rather than compare which of two similar products is better.

### The Tequila Test (practical method)
1. List all the conventional advice on a topic
2. Cross all of it out
3. Write what remains
4. If you can't write anything once it's crossed out, you don't yet have a truly differentiated point of view

### Languaging (the art of naming)
- Two words can change how a category is perceived: car → electric car / romance → military romance
- Cole's experience: he evolved from charging by the word to "choosing the right two words" to define a new category
- In practice: give your unique method / framework a name of its own (Ship 30 itself is a languaging case)

### Views ≠ Success
Cole's hard-won lesson: he accumulated 500M views but earned very little. After moving to a niche (digital writing), views fell but revenue soared. **Quality engagement > exposure volume.**

## 6. The Seven-Step Atomic Essay Method

**Source**: [Atomic Essay Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-an-atomic-essay-a-beginners-guide) (primary)

1. **Pick a Specific Topic**: Use guiding questions to overcome choice anxiety (write about what you're consuming / respond to others / curate resources / teach a how-to / share life lessons)
2. **Define Your Audience**: Three audience buckets: General (relevant to everyone) / Niche (a specific group) / Industry (expert level)
3. **Craft an Intriguing Headline**: It must include the 5 elements: who it's for / what it's about / emotion / promise / number
4. **Outline Key Points**: Deliver exactly what the headline promises (if you say 7 ways, you must give 7 ways)
5. **Expand Main Points**: Add personal stories, research data, and where the idea came from
6. **Edit for Readability**: Capitalize headings, bold key words, use color and emoji strategically, make it visually appealing
7. **Publish & Track Data**: After publishing, track the data and use it to guide what you write next

**Core principle**: "Your essay isn't about you. It's about your readers."

## 8. Dickie Bush's Twitter Growth Strategy

### Sources
- [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)
- [Nathan Barry Interview](https://nathanbarry.com/035-dickie-bush-100000-writing-twitter/) (primary interview)
- [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)
- [Podcast Notes](https://podcastnotes.org/uncategorized/dickie-bush-louis-shulman-kyle-bishop-writing/) (secondary summary)

### Key strategies

**1. Twitter As Idea Refinery**
- Don't treat Twitter as a broadcast channel, treat it as an "idea refinery"
- Post a tweet → watch the data → expand the ones with good data into threads → turn the threads that perform well into newsletters → eventually turn them into products

**2. Only-I-Can-Create Standard**
Before publishing, ask yourself: "Would I want to consume this myself?" and "Would the me from two years ago find this valuable?"

**3. Size of Question = Size of Audience**
- Big question (how to be happier) = big audience but shallow
- Small question (how to grow podcast from 2k to 10k) = small audience but deep
- Strategy: use big questions to attract new people, use small questions to retain them

**4. Bootstrap Initial Audience**
You don't need a large following. Just 2-3 people interested in your content are enough to start a feedback loop.

**5. Writing As Pre-Thinking**
"Writing is pre-thinking future conversations." Writing is not expressing ideas you already have, it is thinking through writing.

**6. Lessons from his starting story**
He started in January 2020. After 9 months his newsletter had only 300 subscribers. He committed to writing a thread every day for 30 days. On day 28, Naval retweeted him and his follower count doubled. Lesson: persist long enough for luck to have a chance to happen.

## 9. Common Writing Mistakes and Fixes (Ship 30 Teaching)

**Source**: [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Don't know what to write | Endless Idea Generator (2-Year Test + 4A) |
| Perfectionism | Aim for "junk" rather than perfection: publish first, iterate later |
| Procrastination | Sacred Hours: put writing on the calendar instead of looking for free time |
| No confidence | Writing itself is how you build confidence; you don't need confidence before you write |
| Impostor syndrome | Don't compete with existing categories, create your own category |
| Not productive enough | Drive output with systems (frameworks), not inspiration |
| Hard to choose a platform | Don't start a personal blog: go to Twitter/Medium/Quora and other places that already have traffic |
| Can't find the time | Make time, don't find time (Sacred Hours) |

## 10. Business Model and Monetization Paths

**Source**: [Snow Leopard Notes](https://www.danielscrivner.com/notes/snow-leopard-nicolas-cole) (secondary detailed notes) + [Passionfroot Interview](https://www.passionfroot.me/creators-on-air/digital-writing-twitter-growth-and-a-7-figure-side-hustle-with-dickie-bush) (primary interview)
