# Richard Feynman Research Materials

## Research date: 2026-04-04
## Research method: 5 parallel agents collecting by dimension + framework distillation

## Source list

### Primary sources (produced directly by Feynman)
- Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman! (1985) — oral autobiography
- What Do You Care What Other People Think? (1988) — sequel + Challenger
- The Pleasure of Finding Things Out (1999) — collection of talks/interviews
- The Character of Physical Law (1965) — Cornell Messenger Lectures
- QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter (1985) — popular science for the public
- The Feynman Lectures on Physics (1963-65) — Caltech undergraduate teaching
- "Cargo Cult Science" (1974) — Caltech commencement address
- "The Value of Science" (1955) — talk to the National Academy of Sciences
- Challenger accident investigation, Appendix F (1986) — Rogers Commission minority report
- Unsent letter to his wife Arline (1946) — private correspondence
- BBC Fun to Imagine series (1983)
- BBC Horizon "The Pleasure of Finding Things Out" (1981)
- The writing on the blackboard at his death (1988)

### Secondary sources (analysis by others)
- James Gleick, Genius: The Life and Science of Richard Feynman (1992) — the most authoritative biography
- Freeman Dyson's various recollections and assessments
- Murray Gell-Mann's assessments and obituary
- Julian Schwinger's methodological criticism
- Hans Bethe's Los Alamos recollections (Web of Stories)
- Paul Halpern, The Quantum Labyrinth — the Wheeler-Feynman relationship
- Gian-Carlo Rota's account of the "12 favorite problems"
- Caltech Letters, Scientific American, The Baffler, and others on the analysis of the gender controversy

### Key quotes (original text)

> "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."
> —— Cargo Cult Science, 1974

> "I can live with doubt and uncertainty and not knowing. I think it's much more interesting to live not knowing than to have answers which might be wrong."
> —— BBC Horizon, 1981

> "You can know the name of that bird in all the languages of the world, but when you're finished, you'll know absolutely nothing whatever about the bird."
> —— Teaching of his father Melville, as retold by Feynman

> "For a successful technology, reality must take precedence over public relations, for nature cannot be fooled."
> —— Challenger Appendix F, 1986

> "Of all its many values, the greatest must be the freedom to doubt."
> —— The Value of Science, 1955

> "What I cannot create, I do not understand."
> —— Handwritten on the blackboard at his death, 1988

> "I'd hate to die twice. It's so boring."
> —— Dying words, 1988

> "P.S. Please excuse my not mailing this — but I don't know your new address."
> —— Closing of the letter to his late wife Arline, 1946

> "He surrounded himself with a cloud of myth, and he spent a great deal of time and energy generating anecdotes about himself."
> —— Murray Gell-Mann's assessment

> "Half genius, half buffoon... later I changed my mind: he was all genius."
> —— Freeman Dyson

### Contradictions and items to verify
- Late in life Dyson re-assessed Feynman as a "great calculator" rather than a "great physicist", a marked change from his early assessments
- Feynman's image as a "casual rebel" vs the "carefully cultivated myth" that Gell-Mann points out
- Feynman advocated "not fooling yourself" vs the autobiography's lack of reflective awareness about certain behaviors
