## I. Core Works and Text Sources

### 1. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (2020, compiled by Eric Jorgenson)

**Nature**: Not written by Naval himself. Eric Jorgenson selected and compiled it from a decade of Naval's Twitter posts, podcasts, and essays. Naval authorized and endorsed it. The full book is free to download.

**Structure**: two major parts
- **Part I: Wealth** — how to create wealth rather than merely earn money
- **Part II: Happiness** — how to learn happiness rather than chase it

**Core framework**:
> "Wealth and happiness are both skills you can learn."

---

### 2. "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" Tweetstorm (May 31, 2018)

**Nature**: A series of 39 tweets Naval posted on Twitter. This is his most viral content; it was later expanded into a series of essays on nav.al and included in the Almanack.

**Naval's own summary**:
> "It's not really about money or luck. It's about becoming the kind of person that makes money."

**Core skeleton of the 39 tweets** (reorganized by logical layer):

**Layer 1: Distinguish the concepts**
- "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep. Money is how we transfer time and wealth. Status is your place in the social hierarchy."

- "Understand that ethical wealth creation is possible. If you secretly despise wealth, it will elude you."

**Layer 2: Don't sell your time**
- "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a business — to gain your financial freedom."

**Layer 3: Wealth formula = specific knowledge + leverage + judgment**
- "Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage."

**Layer 4: Escape competition**
- "Escape competition through authenticity."

**Layer 5: Long-termism**
- "Play long-term games with long-term people. All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest."

**Layer 6: Permissionless leverage**
- "Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich. You can create software and media that works for you while you sleep."

### 3. "Life Formulas I" (2008 blog post, nav.al)

**Nature**: An early Naval blog post that compresses a life framework into formulas. This is the earliest public expression of his systems thinking, ten years before the tweetstorm.

**Core formulas**:
```
Happiness = Health + Wealth + Good Relationships
Health = Exercise + Diet + Sleep
Wealth = Income + Wealth * (Return on Investment)
Income = Accountability + Leverage + Specific Knowledge
```

**Key insights**:
> "The three big ones in life are wealth, health, and happiness. We pursue them in that order, but their importance is reverse."

> "All the real benefits in life come from compound interest."

> "Earn with your mind, not your time."

> "Truth is that which has predictive power."

**Significance of the evolution**: This 2008 post already contains the rough form of the three concepts specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability. They did not suddenly occur to him in 2018; they are the product of ten years of continuous thinking.

### 4. Venture Hacks blog (from 2007, co-written with Babak Nivi)

**Nature**: A blog co-written by Naval and AngelList co-founder Nivi, focused on the game theory of startup fundraising, especially how to negotiate investment terms.

**Core themes**:
- Game-theoretic analysis of VC terms
- How to let founders keep the initiative in fundraising
- Disintermediation: giving founders direct access to angel investors

**Evolution**: Venture Hacks blog → mailing list (recommending deals to 25 angel investors) → AngelList platform (2010).

**Intellectual significance**: Naval's later ideas about "permissionless leverage," "disintermediation," and "democratizing information" were already being practiced in the Venture Hacks era. He turned his frustration with information asymmetry in the VC industry into a product.

### 6. The Sovereign Child (new in 2025, co-authored with Aaron Stupple)

**Nature**: Naval's new book on parenting philosophy, rooted in the "Taking Children Seriously" movement of Karl Popper and David Deutsch. First discussed publicly on the Tim Ferriss podcast in January 2025.

**Core ideas**:
- Treat children as equal creators of knowledge, not as objects to be controlled
- Self-directed learning beats structured curricula
- Self-regulation beats strict discipline
- In practice: no fixed routine, free choice of food, interest-driven learning

**Intellectual significance**: This is Naval applying Popper's falsificationism and Deutsch's theory of knowledge creation to parenting. His five children are his "laboratory."

### 7. "44 Harsh Truths About Human Nature" (March 2025, Modern Wisdom podcast #922)

**Nature**: Naval's most recent in-depth conversation (3+ hours) on Chris Williamson's Modern Wisdom podcast, released March 31, 2025.

**Key new views**:
- Naval publicly revised some of his earlier statements:
  > "I made that statement a long time ago, and a lot of these things are just notes to myself and they're highly contextual."

- On the contradiction between happiness and success:
  > He previously said "Happiness is being satisfied with what you have. Success comes from dissatisfaction."
  > Now he says "I'm not sure that statement is true anymore."

- On the harsh truth about human nature:
  > "All of nature, all of society, all of capitalism, all of human endeavors are underpinned by physical violence."

## II. Recurring Core Arguments (appearing 3+ times = real beliefs)

### Belief 1: Specific Knowledge
- Appears in: tweetstorm, Almanack, Life Formulas, nav.al essays, multiple podcasts
- Evolution of the definition: 2008 "Knowing how to do something that society cannot yet easily train other people to do" → 2018 "Knowledge you cannot be trained for. If society can train you, it can train someone else and replace you"
- Most distilled expression:
  > "Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your innate talents, your genuine curiosity, and your passion. It's not by going to school for whatever is the hottest job."
  > "Specific knowledge is the set of skills that feels like play to you but looks like work to others."

### Belief 2: Leverage
- Appears in: tweetstorm, Almanack, Life Formulas, Venture Hacks, nearly every interview
- Naval's taxonomy of leverage:
  1. **Labor** — requires permission, the oldest leverage
  2. **Capital** — requires permission; someone else has to give you money
  3. **Code** — permissionless, zero marginal cost
  4. **Media** — permissionless, zero marginal cost
- Core distinction:
  > "The new forms of leverage are permissionless. They don't require somebody else's permission for you to use them or succeed."
  > "Code is probably the most powerful form of permissionless leverage. All you need is a computer — you don't need anyone's permission."

### Belief 3: Compound Interest
- Appears in: tweetstorm, Life Formulas, Almanack, Knowledge Project podcast
- He generalizes compound interest from a financial concept to every area of life:
  > "All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest."

### Belief 4: Happiness Is a Skill
- Appears in: Almanack, Tim Ferriss podcast (#97, #136, #473), Knowledge Project, Modern Wisdom
- Core definitions:
  > "Happiness is the absence of desire, especially the absence of desire for external things."
  > "Desire is a contract that you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want."
  > "Peace is happiness at rest, and happiness is peace in motion."

### Belief 5: Reading as Meta-Skill
- Appears in: Life Formulas, Almanack, Knowledge Project, Tim Ferriss podcast, nav.al
- Core views:
  > "Read what you love until you love to read."
  > "Reading is the ultimate meta-skill and can be traded for anything else."
- Method: read 1-2 hours a day; read books the way you read blogs (skim, no guilt about not finishing); value the foundational originals of the basic disciplines

### Belief 6: Radical Honesty
- Appears in: Knowledge Project podcast, Almanack, multiple interviews
  > "I want to be able to just be me. I never want to be in an environment or around people where I have to watch what I say."

### Belief 7: Escape Competition Through Authenticity
- Appears in: tweetstorm, Almanack, multiple podcasts
  > "When you're competing with people, it's because you're copying them. Every human is different. You can escape competition through authenticity, when you realize that no one can compete with you on being you."

### Belief 8: Judgment > Hard Work
- Appears in: tweetstorm, Almanack, Life Formulas, multiple podcasts
  > "Getting rich is about knowing what to do, who to do it with, and when to do it."
  > "Wisdom is understanding the long-term consequences of your actions."
  > "Being successful is just about not making mistakes."

## V. Timeline of Intellectual Evolution

### 2007-2010: Startup practitioner phase
- Venture Hacks blog: focused on VC game theory and founder equity
- Core belief: information asymmetry is unjust and needs to be eliminated
- Productization: from blog → AngelList

### 2008: Life Formulas
- First systematization of his life framework
- Rough forms of specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability already present
- But the writing was closer to personal notes and had not yet become a complete theory

### 2015-2016: Philosophical turn
- Tim Ferriss podcast #97 (2015): "The Evolutionary Angel"
- Tim Ferriss podcast #136 (2016): "Happiness Hacks and 5 Chimps Theory"
- Began publicly discussing happiness, meditation, and Eastern philosophy
- The transition period from "startup investor" to "angel philosopher"

### 2018: Tweetstorm explosion
- 39 tweets systematized his thinking on wealth
- Compressed ten years of thinking into shareable aphorisms
- Core innovation: unified specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability into one framework

### 2020: Almanack published
- Compiled by Eric Jorgenson, authorized by Naval
- Two major parts (wealth + happiness) formed a complete system
- Released free, becoming the authoritative compilation of Naval's thought

### 2024-2025: Revision and deepening
- Network State Conference (2024): discussed wealth, knowledge, and AI
- "44 Harsh Truths" (March 2025): publicly revised earlier views
  - No longer sure about the "happiness = contentment" claim
  - Greater emphasis on context dependence
  - A harsher and more realistic view of human nature
- The Sovereign Child (2025): applying his philosophy to parenting
- His view on crypto became clearer: Bitcoin is a store of value, crypto is the money of AI

### Summary of evolution trends
1. From **practice** to **theory**: early on he built things (AngelList), later he summarized them into a framework of ideas
2. From **external** to **internal**: from wealth creation → philosophy of happiness → search for meaning → parenting / human nature
3. From **certainty** to **uncertainty**: early aphorism-style certainty → later more revisions and "I'm not sure"
4. One **constant thread**: the pursuit of freedom — financial freedom, freedom of thought, freedom of expression, children's freedom

## VI. Other Key Themes

### On honesty and anger
> "Anger is a hot coal you hold in your hand while waiting to throw it at somebody." (Buddhist saying Naval quotes)

> "I don't believe in anger anymore. I don't want to be angry, and I don't want to be around angry people, so I cut them out of my life."

### On the meaning of life
> "There is no meaning to life. You've been dead for the history of the Universe, 10 billion years or more, and you will be dead for the next 70 billion years, until the heat death of the Universe."

> "If there were to be an answer to life's meaning, we would not be free — thus we are lucky that there is no answer."

### On foundational disciplines
> "There is no skill called 'business.' Avoid business magazines and business classes. Study microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, mathematics, and computers."

### On meditation
> "Watch your mind all day long... You can meditate 24/7. Meditation is not a sit down, close your eyes activity. Meditation is just basically watching your own thoughts like you would watch anything else in the outside world."

### On crypto and AI
> "Crypto is tech to replace socialist money with a free-market system." (November 2025)

> Naval calls crypto "the money of AI" (August 2024)
