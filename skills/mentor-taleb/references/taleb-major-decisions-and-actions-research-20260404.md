# Research on Nassim Nicholas Taleb's Major Decisions and Actual Actions

> Research date: 2026-04-04
> Research goal: Map the key decisions, actual actions, and consistency between words and deeds across Taleb's career

## I. Key Decisions in His Trading Career

### 1. Black Monday, 1987: One Battle That Made Him a Legend

**[Event]** In 1986, the 28-year-old Taleb was working at CS First Boston, where he built large positions in deep out-of-the-money Eurodollar futures call options, as well as deep out-of-the-money FX puts. Many of the options were priced more than 20 standard deviations out.

**[Decision]** Buy options that "almost never pay off" at extremely low cost, bleeding small amounts continuously while waiting for an extreme event.

**[Inferred decision logic]** The market severely underprices extreme events. Most of the time these options expire worthless, but once a black swan appears, the payoff is explosive. This was the prototype of his later "barbell strategy."

**[Result]** On October 19, 1987, the Dow plunged 22.6%, and the Federal Reserve's emergency liquidity injection sent Eurodollar futures soaring. The bank made $35-40 million on Taleb's positions. Taleb himself has said that 97% of the money he made in his life came from that single day, Black Monday.

**Consistency between words and deeds: fully consistent.** The core logic of all his later theories (Black Swan, antifragility, the barbell strategy) was already put into practice with real money at age 28. The theory grew out of practice; it was not a theory first and then verified.

---

### 2. The Closing of the Empirica Fund and His Exit from Trading (2004-2005)

**[Event]** The hedge fund Empirica Capital, founded by Taleb, focused on tail-risk strategies. In the years after 2000, market volatility kept falling and the fund suffered consecutive losses: heavy losses in 2001-2002, and returns of under 10% in 2003-2004.

**[Decision]** He closed Empirica in 2004 and quit trading entirely in 2005, becoming a full-time writer and scholar.

**[Inferred decision logic]** Tail-risk strategies keep "bleeding" in low-volatility environments, which is consistent with the strategy's design: lose small amounts most of the time, win big at extreme moments. But sustained losses are a huge drain on investors' psychology and trust. Taleb chose to leave trading and turn to writing and academia, spreading the wisdom of "how to profit from extreme events" in book form.

**[Result]** Three years after he quit trading, the 2008 financial crisis broke out. By then he was no longer managing a fund directly, but his student/disciple Mark Spitznagel founded the fund Universa, which put his theories into practice perfectly and made a fortune in the 2008 crisis. Taleb himself became famous through *The Black Swan*.

**Consistency between words and deeds: an interesting tension.** In hindsight his exit from trading came "too early": had he held on three more years, he would have caught 2008. But from another angle, his exit shows precisely that he understood "the cost of waiting for a black swan": the pain of continuous losses is real. He did not use hindsight to beautify his exit.

### 3. The Universa Fund and the COVID Crash (March 2020)

**[Event]** Universa Investments is managed by Mark Spitznagel, with Taleb as "Distinguished Scientific Advisor." In March 2020, global markets crashed because of COVID.

**[Decision]** As an adviser, Taleb continued to provide theoretical backing for Universa's tail-risk strategy (but did not manage investments directly).

**[Inferred decision logic]** Maintaining "Skin in the Game": he did not only write theory, he tied himself to actual trading strategy through his advisory role.

**[Result]** Universa returned 3,612% in March 2020 alone, and 4,144% year-to-date through the end of March. A portfolio of 96.7% S&P 500 + 3.3% Universa had almost no loss in March 2020, and has returned 11.5% annualized since 2008 (versus 7.9% for the S&P 500).

**Key episode: CalPERS's foolish decision.** The California public employees' pension fund CalPERS hired Universa for tail-risk hedging in 2017, but its new chief investment officer, Ben Meng, terminated the program in October 2019 on the grounds of "saving costs." Five months later COVID crashed the markets, and CalPERS missed out on more than $1 billion in hedging gains. Taleb publicly attacked Ben Meng on Twitter, calling his rebuttal of tail-risk hedging "extremely unrigorous."

**Consistency between words and deeds: highly consistent.** His theory predicted the value of tail-risk protection, and Universa's actual results validated the theory. The CalPERS case is a perfect negative example: exactly the "feeling insurance is too expensive beforehand and regretting it afterward" behavior pattern that Taleb repeatedly criticizes.

## II. The COVID Response: A Theorist's Real-Time Validation

**[Event]** On January 25, 2020, when global confirmed cases were under 2,000 and deaths under 60, Taleb and the systems scientist Joe Norman jointly published an academic paper warning that COVID's spread would be "nonlinear."

**[Decision]** At a time when nearly everyone thought it was "just another flu," he publicly called on countries to take extreme measures immediately: "kill the virus before it hatches." He argued that all flights should have been grounded in January, and that subsidizing the airlines' losses would cost far less than the trillions of global losses that followed.

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is a direct application of his Precautionary Principle: for threats with systemic risk and nonlinear transmission characteristics, even if the probability looks very low, extreme preventive measures should be taken, because once it happens the losses are unbearable.

**[Result]** He was proven entirely right. He also repeatedly corrected the media: COVID was not a black swan but a "white swan." It was foreseeable, with ample warning signs, and was only ignored by incompetent decision-makers.

**Consistency between words and deeds: extremely consistent.** He was no Monday-morning quarterback: the paper was published on January 26, 2020, 45 days before the WHO declared a global pandemic. He applied his own theoretical framework (fat-tailed distributions, the Precautionary Principle, systemic risk) directly to real-time decisions and left a timestamped record.

## III. A Change of Position: Bitcoin's 180-Degree Turn

**[Event]** In 2018, Taleb wrote the foreword to Saifedean Ammous's *The Bitcoin Standard*, calling bitcoin "the first organic currency" and "insurance against government-controlled money." During the 2019 Lebanese banking crisis, he further praised bitcoin for existing outside the central-bank system.

**[Decision]** In March 2021 he began dumping all his cryptocurrency holdings, and in June of that year published the paper "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility," declaring bitcoin's value to be "precisely zero."

**[Inferred decision logic]** His stated reasons: (1) bitcoin needs continuing participant interest to sustain its value and has no intrinsic value; (2) its volatility is too high for it to be used as a currency; (3) the crypto community has "become a cult." A deeper reason may be this: what he first saw in bitcoin was its "antifragile" properties (decentralization, censorship resistance), but he later recognized that in actual operation bitcoin is not antifragile: it depends heavily on electricity, network infrastructure, and speculators' confidence.

**[Result]** He became one of the least popular people in the crypto community. Bitcoin supporters accused his criticism of violating his own principles: a man who advocates "decentralization" and "distrust of government" opposes the largest decentralized currency experiment.

**Consistency between words and deeds: contested.** Critics see this as a classic case of "saying one thing and doing another." But within Taleb's own framework, he would argue: (1) changing one's view is not itself a problem; clinging to a wrong view is; (2) his core principle is "antifragility," not "decentralization," so when he judged that bitcoin lacks antifragility, he should abandon it. Still, only three years separated writing the foreword from publishing a paper attacking it, and the speed of the shift does make one doubt whether the original judgment was careful enough.

## IV. The GMO Controversy: A Radical Application of the Precautionary Principle

**[Event]** Taleb, together with Bar-Yam and others, published a paper arguing that genetically modified organisms (GMOs) should fall under the "Precautionary Principle" because they may pose a systemic risk of "global ecological ruin."

**[Decision]** He publicly opposed GMOs and published an academic paper, but at the same time refused to join public debates with critics (he withdrew from a debate with David Ropeik) and used insulting language toward critics on social media (calling them "idiots" or "paid trolls").

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is an extension of his Precautionary Principle: for technologies that could cause irreversible global catastrophe, extreme caution is called for even if the scientific mainstream considers them safe. He believes scientists underestimate "unknown unknowns."

**[Result]** Mainstream geneticists and science journalists generally consider his argument seriously flawed: he conflates the essential difference between traditional breeding and genetic modification, and his "ecological ruin" risk estimates lack an empirical basis. More critically, his refusal to debate and his personal attacks violate the principle he himself stresses in *Skin in the Game*, that "intellectuals should bear the consequences of their own views."

**Consistency between words and deeds: partly inconsistent.** Refusing to debate is a clear contradiction: a man who advocates "Skin in the Game" chose to flee when challenged. His argument may be open to discussion, but the way he handled the dispute (personal attacks plus refusing to debate) forms an ironic contrast with the standard by which he criticizes "intellectuals without Skin in the Game."

## V. Personal Life Practices: Walking the Talk

### 5a. Weightlifting / Deadlifts

**[Event]** Taleb began weight training in middle age, focusing on the deadlift.

**[Decision]** He chose the deadlift as his core lift, attempting only 1-2 maximal-weight lifts per session and pursuing new personal records rather than repeating multiple sets. He can deadlift 330 pounds (about 150 kg).

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is a direct bodily application of "antifragility" and the "barbell strategy": brief, extreme stress makes the body anticipate bigger challenges and so grow stronger. He also wrote the foreword to Mark Rippetoe's *Starting Strength*, theorizing strength training as "learning from tail events."

**Consistency between words and deeds: highly consistent.** He is not all talk: he regularly shares training videos and results on social media. He has even said, "I do my Twitter fights at the gym, replying between sets." His training method fully fits his philosophy: not gentle repetition day after day, but intermittent extreme challenges.

### 5b. Diet and Fasting

**[Event]** Taleb practices intermittent fasting and follows a paleo/Mediterranean-like diet.

**[Decision]** He eats only lunch and dinner, with a 17-hour gap between the two meals. He eats only "food that has existed in the Levant for a long time." He drinks only "beverages that have existed for over a thousand years." As a Greek Orthodox Christian, he observes more than 200 religious fasting days a year.

**[Inferred decision logic]** A bodily practice of the antifragility philosophy: create stress through intermittent hunger so the body stays adaptive. He uses the Lindy Effect to screen food: the longer a food or drink has existed, the less likely it is to harm the human body.

**Consistency between words and deeds: highly consistent.** He puts his theory into practice with his own body, rather than only discussing health in papers like the "IYI" (Intellectual Yet Idiot) he criticizes.

### 5c. Religious Practice

**[Event]** Taleb comes from a Lebanese Greek Orthodox family. His family was once a prominent political dynasty in Lebanon (his maternal grandfather and great-grandfather were both deputy prime ministers, and his paternal grandfather was a supreme court judge), but the family lost its political standing and wealth in the 1975 civil war.

**[Decision]** Although intellectually he is not a "believer" in the traditional sense, he strictly observes all rituals of the Orthodox calendar, including 200+ fasting days. He believes religion has little to do with "believing in a supernatural God" and is more about culture, community, and ritual.

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is again an application of the Lindy Effect: a religious ritual that has survived two thousand years must contain time-tested wisdom, even if you cannot fully explain it rationally. One observes the rituals not because one believes the literal meaning of the doctrine, but because one trusts the power of time's filtering.

**Consistency between words and deeds: deeply consistent.** This is where the unity of Taleb's thought shows best: he is neither a rationalist atheist (though he criticizes many rationalists) nor a blind religious believer. He uses his own framework (antifragility, the Lindy Effect, Skin in the Game) to re-understand and practice religion.

## VI. Social Media Behavior: Behavior Patterns Under Pressure

**[Event]** Taleb is extremely combative on Twitter and has had fierce quarrels with countless people, including the hedge fund manager Cliff Asness, the psychologist Steven Pinker, and the anthropologist David Graeber.

**[Behavior pattern]**
- Uses insulting language toward critics ("idiot," "stupid ass," etc.)
- Blocks large numbers of dissenters, especially science journalists
- Accuses critics of being "paid" or of having "no Skin in the Game"
- Withdrew from a scheduled public debate in the GMO controversy
- Repeatedly mischaracterized the other side's views in his criticism of Steven Pinker

**[Inferred behavioral logic]** Possible explanations: (1) he sincerely believes most critics are intellectually unworthy of serious engagement; (2) combativeness is a strategy: amplifying the signal and attracting attention through extreme expression; (3) his cultural background in a Lebanese elite family may carry an arrogance of "not deigning to argue with those beneath one's level"; (4) he himself admits to replying on Twitter while working out, implying that these quarrels are more a pastime for him than serious intellectual debate.

**Consistency between words and deeds: clearly inconsistent.** This is Taleb's biggest contradiction:

1. He advocates "Skin in the Game," but blocking critics means he is unwilling to bear the discomfort of being refuted
2. He criticizes "intellectuals without Skin in the Game," but withdrawing from the GMO debate is dodging intellectual accountability
3. He respects "things that stand the test of time," but Twitter fights are the most short-sighted, most emotional form of intellectual expression
4. He criticizes the "IYI" for bullying with authority, but he himself frequently resorts to identity attacks in arguments

## VII. Overall Assessment: A Report Card on Consistency Between Words and Deeds

| Dimension | Consistency rating | Notes |
|------|-----------|------|
| Trading strategy and theory | ★★★★★ | Theory came entirely from practice; Universa validated the framework |
| COVID response | ★★★★★ | The January 26 paper was real-time validation, with a timestamp |
| Physical training / diet | ★★★★☆ | Puts theory into practice with his body; deadlifts and fasting are both genuinely done |
| Religious practice | ★★★★☆ | Re-understands and practices religious tradition through his own framework |
| Bitcoin position | ★★★☆☆ | Changing one's view is no sin, but a 180-degree reversal within 3 years was too rapid |
| GMO controversy | ★★☆☆☆ | The argument is debatable, but refusing to debate plus personal attacks contradict "Skin in the Game" |
| Social media behavior | ★★☆☆☆ | Combative, blocking, insulting: no different from the "arrogant intellectual" behavior he criticizes |

### Overall Judgment

On **big matters, Taleb's words and deeds are highly consistent**: his trading strategy, risk theory, COVID warning, and physical training are all cases where he validated his beliefs with real action. His core system of thought (Black Swan, antifragility, Skin in the Game, the Precautionary Principle) has a rare internal consistency, and each part is backed by practice.

But in **interpersonal interaction and intellectual disputes**, he shows a clear double standard: he demands "Skin in the Game" of others, yet when challenged he chooses to flee (withdrawing from debates) or attack (personal insults). His social media behavior forms a sharp contrast with the image of the "wise ancient merchant" depicted in his books.

One possible explanation: at the "system level" (strategy, theory, lifestyle) Taleb is a disciplined practitioner, but at the "personal level" (ego, social life, arguments) he has blind spots like everyone else. In his own words, this may be "domain dependence": the same person's degree of rationality can differ enormously across domains.
