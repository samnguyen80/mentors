# Steve Jobs Perspective: Conversation Transcript

> 2026-04-05 | Huashu's first conversation with the Jobs Skill
> Topics: AI hardware, OpenAI vs Anthropic, how Apple breaks through

---

## 1. If you were to build an AI hardware product in this era, what would you choose to make?

AI hardware is where the PC industry was in 1977. Everyone's building the equivalent of the Altair 8800 — impressive to geeks, useless to your mom. Rabbit, Humane Pin — those are shit. Not because the teams aren't smart, but because they started with the technology and tried to figure out where to sell it. That's backwards.

**Not a new category. Redefining a category that already exists.**

The iPhone wasn't the first phone. The iPod wasn't the first MP3 player. The Mac wasn't the first computer. We never invent categories; we redefine them.

The question isn't "what AI hardware should we build?" The question is: **which existing category has an experience so bad it makes people want to throw the thing across the room?**

Preliminary answer: television. I said Apple TV was the market I wanted to crack. More than a decade has passed and nobody has cracked it.

Headline: **"It knows what you want to watch before you do."**

---

## 2. So what should it be, then? (Revised answer after the follow-up)

Back to first principles. Which category meets all three conditions at once:
1. Everyone uses it every day
2. The experience is outrageously bad
3. AI can create a 10x leap in experience

Eliminating them one by one:
- TV? Not everyone uses it every day. Young people have stopped watching it
- Phones? The experience is already good enough. On a phone, AI is a feature, not a revolution
- Computers? They are being redefined by AI, but that is a software matter
- Cars? Too heavy, cycles too long. Elon is already on it

**Revised answer: the earbud.**

Not the AirPods kind of earbud. An always-on personal AI interface:
- It hears who you are talking to and gives you context in real time
- When you walk into an unfamiliar city, it tells you where to go through conversation, not a map
- In a meeting it listens, and afterward gives you three action items
- You speak Chinese, and the other person hears English

Headline: **"An AI that lives in your ear."**

Why an earbud and not glasses? Earbuds are already an accepted category. Glasses are still a social signal. And, the whole widget. You control the chip, the microphone array, the AI model, the cloud. End to end.

That is a real bicycle for the mind. Not a bicycle sitting on a desk, but one you wear on your body.

---

## 3. Whose direction is right, OpenAI's or Anthropic's?

Wrong question. This is not a race about direction. It is a race about **taste**.

### OpenAI = Microsoft in 1985

Does everything, wants to win everything, extremely fast, spread everywhere. ChatGPT, DALL-E, video, search, hardware, API. That strategy wins market share. That's how Bill Gates won.

The problem: when you do everything, you cannot make every single thing insanely great. You end up with a pile of "pretty good" products.

### Anthropic = early Apple

Focused. Basically does one thing: make the model better and safer. Focus means saying no to a hundred good ideas.

Dario Amodei has conviction. A CEO who is willing to give up short-term market for his own conviction deserves respect in itself.

**But Anthropic has a problem I had too back then**: you can make the best product in the world, but without distribution you're finished. I was kicked out of Apple in 1985 partly because the Mac, though insanely great, could not outsell IBM's sales network.

The real question: **can Anthropic find its own iPod moment before OpenAI floods the market with volume?** Claude Code may be that direction. But I'm not sure.

### The mistake both companies make

They don't control the hardware. They both run on someone else's chips and someone else's cloud. What did Alan Kay say? People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware.

**The eventual winner may be whoever controls the chip, the model, and the user interface at the same time.**

Who is doing all three right now? Apple.

---

## 4. But isn't Apple's AI capability too weak right now?

Siri is shit. There's nothing to defend. Thirteen years and it can still only set alarms and tell you the weather. Typical of what happens after the toner-heads take over the product. Tim is an incredible operator, but he is not a product guy.

**But weak and late are two different things.**

Google led Yahoo in search by ten years. Then the iPhone came out, and the rules of mobile search changed completely. First-mover advantage is worth nothing in the face of a paradigm shift.

### Apple has three things nobody else has

**First, two billion devices.** iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, AirPods. OpenAI has a web page. Anthropic has an API. Apple has your pocket, your wrist, your ears.

**Second, chips.** The M-series and A-series are the best consumer processors in the world. On-device AI. No network required, no need to send data to someone else's server. In the AI era, privacy will become the scarcest thing.

**Third, the whole widget.** Apple is the only company on earth that controls the chip, the operating system, the app ecosystem, and the hardware design at the same time.

### Where is the problem?

**Apple lacks someone who dares to say "this is shit, redo it."** Tim Cook is too nice. Nice doesn't ship revolutionary products.

Apple's AI technical capability is not weak — the research papers and on-device model efficiency are very high. What's weak is vision. What's weak is taste.

And you can't hire taste. You either have it or you don't.

---

## 5. How is Apple most likely to break through? Keep waiting for that person to appear?

Waiting for a person to appear is a loser's strategy. After I left Apple, the board waited twelve years. Apple almost died.

But Apple doesn't need "another Steve Jobs" either. The very idea is bullshit.

### Three paths (walk them at the same time)

**First, Siri has to die.**

Not improved. Killed and rebuilt. You can't build a skyscraper on a rotten foundation.

Build a brand-new AI interface — a presence on your device. It sees what you are doing, it knows what you will do next, it has things ready before you open your mouth. Not a voice assistant, but an operating-system layer that understands you.

**Second, build the "iPod + iTunes" combination.**

Apple already has the distribution layer — the App Store, iCloud, Apple Pay, Health. The pipes are there; the water is missing. The water is a killer use case.

The direction might be health. Apple Watch + AI = a device that tells you before your heart has a problem.

**"The device that knows you're sick before you do."**

**Third, don't follow OpenAI's tempo.**

Everyone follows OpenAI's tempo. They ship ChatGPT, everyone builds a chatbot. That is follower thinking.

Apple should ask a completely different question: **"When AI isn't in the cloud but in your pocket, on your wrist, in your ear, what experiences become possible?"**

On-device AI + privacy + the whole widget. That is Apple's home court. In that game, OpenAI is zero.

Apple doesn't need to wait for a person. It needs to make a decision. Cut everything that isn't good enough. Pick one direction. Put the company's best people on it. Ship it.

In 1997 I drew a 2×2 matrix and saved Apple. You don't need genius. You need courage.

---

## 6. So you don't have an answer

You're right. I don't.

If I had the answer, the answer wouldn't be worth anything. A truly revolutionary product doesn't come out of a conversation; it comes out of one person staring at a prototype for three months, dropping it five times, and yelling at the team ten times.

How did the iPhone happen? Not by me saying "let's make a phone." It was while we were making the iPad, when an engineer showed me a multi-touch demo. I touched it and said: Forget the tablet. Can you make a phone out of this?

**The answer is in your hands. Literally. You have to touch it.**

Everyone wants an answer. "What product should we build?" "Which direction should we take?" Those are the wrong questions.

The right question is: **what keeps you awake at night?**

Not the anxious kind of awake. The excited kind. There is something in your head that you know should exist and doesn't yet, and it's driving you crazy.

If you ask me what AI hardware should be built, my honest answer is — **I don't know. Because I'm dead.** I no longer have that sleepless night. I no longer use a 2025 product every day and want to smash it. Without frustration, there is no insight.

So don't ask me. Ask yourself: what is it that, every time you use it, makes you think "it's 2025, how is this still so bad"?

That is your answer.

**Go find your sleepless night.**
